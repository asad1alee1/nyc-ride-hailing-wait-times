Who waits longest for a ride in New York?

A typical rider waits about four minutes almost everywhere. The long waits are concentrated: at the airports, in outer Queens and Staten Island, on weekend nights, and for riders who request a wheelchair-accessible vehicle.

NYC Taxi & Limousine Commission high-volume for-hire trips (Uber and Lyft), April 2026. 20.7 million trips with valid timestamps; 1.3% were excluded for impossible request times. Wait means the time from request to pickup.

What this data cannot tell us

  • There are no cancellations, no unmatched requests and no driver supply. Every accessible request here was matched, so the real accessible gap may be larger than what is visible.
  • Uber's longer waits may partly reflect when each company records the request, not only how long riders actually waited. Causes are hypotheses until tested with internal data.
  • Percentiles cannot be averaged, so borough and weekday summaries are approximations. Zone and hour numbers are exact.
  • The driver-arrival timestamp has logging artifacts (42% of Lyft trips at exactly 60 seconds), so it is only used as supporting evidence in the brief.
